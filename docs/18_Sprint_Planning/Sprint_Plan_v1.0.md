# 1. Purpose

This document defines the sprint planning methodology, execution process, ceremonies, team responsibilities, and release cadence for AI TradeQ.

It provides a standardized framework for iterative software delivery using Agile Scrum practices.

---

# 2. Agile Principles

The project shall follow:

- Iterative Development
- Incremental Delivery
- Continuous Feedback
- Customer Value First
- Cross-functional Collaboration
- Continuous Improvement
- Transparency

---

# 3. Sprint Duration

Standard Sprint Length:

**2 Weeks (10 Working Days)**

Each sprint shall produce a potentially releasable product increment.

---

# 4. Sprint Lifecycle

Sprint Planning

↓

↓

Daily Standups

↓

Code Review

↓

Testing

↓

Sprint Review

↓

Sprint Retrospective

↓

Next Sprint Planning

---

# 5. Sprint Planning

Planning activities include:

- Backlog refinement
- Story selection
- Capacity estimation
- Dependency review
- Risk identification
- Sprint goal definition

Only "Ready" backlog items may enter a sprint.

---

# 6. Sprint Goal

Every sprint shall define:

- Primary objective
- Expected deliverables
- Acceptance criteria
- Success metrics

The sprint goal shall remain stable throughout the sprint unless formally approved for change.

---

# 7. Daily Standup

Daily standups shall cover:

- What was completed yesterday?
- What will be completed today?
- Are there any blockers?

Target duration:

15 minutes.

---

# 8. Sprint Review

The sprint review shall include:

- Feature demonstrations
- Acceptance validation
- Stakeholder feedback
- Backlog updates

Only completed work shall be demonstrated.

---

# 9. Sprint Retrospective

The team shall discuss:

- What went well?
- What can improve?
- What actions will be taken next sprint?

Improvement actions shall be tracked.

---

# 10. Capacity Planning

Sprint capacity shall consider:

- Team availability
- Holidays
- Meetings
- Technical debt
- Production support
- Planned leave

Story commitment shall not exceed available capacity.

---

# 11. Definition of Sprint Success

A sprint is successful when:

- Sprint goal achieved
- Committed stories completed
- Quality gates passed
- Documentation updated
- No critical defects introduced

---

# 12. Release Planning

Releases shall be planned based on:

- Sprint outcomes
- Business priorities
- Quality readiness
- Security approval
- Production readiness

Multiple sprints may contribute to a single release.

---

# 13. Team Responsibilities

## Product Owner

- Prioritize backlog
- Define sprint goals
- Accept completed work

## Scrum Master

- Facilitate ceremonies
- Remove blockers
- Improve team effectiveness

## Development Team

- Deliver committed work
- Maintain code quality
- Update documentation

## QA Team

- Validate acceptance criteria
- Execute testing
- Report defects

## DevOps Team

- Maintain CI/CD
- Support deployments
- Monitor environments

---

# 14. Sprint Metrics

The project shall monitor:

- Sprint Velocity
- Burndown Chart
- Story Completion Rate
- Defect Leakage
- Escaped Defects
- Build Success Rate
- Lead Time
- Cycle Time

---

# 15. Risks

Sprint risks include:

- Scope creep
- Blocked dependencies
- Unplanned production issues
- Team availability
- External service failures

Mitigation actions shall be documented during planning.

---

# 16. Acceptance Criteria

This document is complete when:

- Sprint lifecycle is defined.
- Scrum ceremonies are documented.
- Capacity planning is established.
- Sprint success criteria are approved.
- Metrics are defined.
